QuickShot — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-26

QuickShot is a Chrome extension that takes screenshots of the tab you are looking at (the visible area, the whole page, or an area you select), lets you edit them, saves them as images or PDF, and can record your screen.

What QuickShot does with your screenshots

When you press a capture button or shortcut, QuickShot takes the picture inside your browser. Depending on your setting it then either saves the file to your Downloads folder straight away, or opens it in an editor tab so you can crop, draw and blur before you save. If you tick "Also copy to clipboard", it also puts the image on your clipboard. Screenshots are never uploaded or sent to a server.

To show the picture in the editor tab, QuickShot keeps it temporarily in your browser's own storage (IndexedDB) on your device, together with the page title. The page address is kept only if you switched on the address and date option. The stored copy is deleted as soon as you save the picture, copy it, or press Discard. If you do none of those, it is deleted after one hour at the latest: a timer (Chrome's alarms feature) checks every 15 minutes while the browser is open, everything is cleared when the browser starts, and only the three newest are ever kept. The editor tab keeps working from memory until you close it. Edits (crop, notes, blur) only change the copy you save.

Screen recording

The recorder page uses Chrome's own screen picker. You choose a tab, a window or your whole screen, and Chrome shows its own "sharing" banner while it records. If you tick the microphone or camera option, Chrome asks for that permission at that moment; QuickShot does not ask for them otherwise. The recording is written in small pieces to your browser's local storage (IndexedDB) while it runs, then joined into one file you can preview and download. The temporary pieces are deleted as soon as you download or delete the recording, when you close the recorder tab, and when the browser starts; the same timer deletes the pieces of an unfinished recording that has not been written to for six hours. If the pieces cannot be written (for example the disk is full), the recording stops, the screen, camera and microphone are released and what was stored is deleted. Recordings, microphone sound and camera pictures are never uploaded or sent anywhere. What gets recorded depends on what you pick in Chrome's picker; sound from the computer is only included if your system, Chrome and your choice in the picker allow it.

What is stored

Your settings, saved in your browser with chrome.storage.local: format, quality, what happens after a capture, whether to hide fixed bars, whether to add an address and date line, PDF paper size and margins, whether to copy to the clipboard, the countdown delay, and a short status line ("Saved") shown in the popup. QuickShot has no account system, no server, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.

Optional address and date line

If you switch on "Add the page address and date under each image", the address of the page you captured (without the part after "#") and the date are written as a small line under the picture you save. It is off by default. The address may include text after a "?", so leave it off for private pages you plan to share.

What is sent over the network

Nothing. QuickShot makes no network requests and loads no remote code. The only web address it ever opens is the uninstall page described below, and only when you remove the extension.

Page access

QuickShot only works on the tab you are using, and only after you click its icon or use its shortcut (Chrome's "activeTab" permission). It does not run on pages in the background and it does not read page text. For full-page capture it briefly scrolls the page (or its main scrolling area or a same-site frame), hides fixed or sticky bars unless you turn that off in Settings, then puts everything back. For "Select area" it shows a selection box on the page, and for a delayed capture a small countdown badge; both disappear before the picture is taken. Frames from other sites cannot be scrolled by an extension, so only their visible part is captured, and QuickShot says so.

Uninstall page

When you remove QuickShot, Chrome opens a page on our policy site (startuptalkyindia.github.io). The address contains only the extension name, its version and its ID. Nothing is sent from that page: it shows an optional "what went wrong?" form that opens an email draft you can choose to send. The site host may keep ordinary web access logs.

Rating reminder

After at least 7 days and 10 screenshots, the QuickShot popup (never a website, never a notification, never the editor or recorder) may show a small card asking for a rating on the Chrome Web Store. It is shown the same way to everyone, at most twice, and you can dismiss it for good. It uses local counters only (install date, number of screenshots, whether you dismissed it), stored in your browser and never sent anywhere.

What we do not do

We do not sell or share data, use it for advertising, or transfer it to anyone. We do not receive any of your data.

Your control

Remove all saved settings and temporary data by removing the extension. Delete screenshots, PDFs and recordings like any other file in your Downloads folder.
