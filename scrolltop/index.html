ScrollTop — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-19 (version 0.2.1)

ScrollTop is a Chrome extension that adds a back-to-top button (and an optional scroll-to-bottom arrow and reading-progress ring) to web pages.

What ScrollTop reads

To do its job, ScrollTop runs on web pages (http and https) and reads only the page's scroll position and the size of its scrolling areas. It does not read the text of pages, forms, passwords, cookies, links or anything you type. It runs only in the main page, not in embedded frames.

What is stored, and where

Only your own settings and the rating-reminder counters, saved in your browser (chrome.storage.local) on your device: which side the button sits on, the list of sites where you switched the button off, and whether the scroll-to-bottom arrow and progress ring are shown. Removing the extension deletes them.

What is sent over the network

Nothing. ScrollTop makes no network requests. It has no server, no account system, no analytics and no advertising. We do not receive any of your data.

Uninstall page

When you remove ScrollTop, Chrome opens a page on our policy site (startuptalkyindia.github.io). The address contains only the extension name, its version and its ID. The page sends nothing; it shows a short "what went wrong?" form that opens an email draft you may choose to send. The site host may keep ordinary access logs.

Rating reminder

ScrollTop keeps three local counters in your browser: when it was installed, how many times you used the button or shortcuts, and whether you dismissed the reminder. After at least 7 days and 10 uses, the toolbar popup (never a website) shows one small card asking you to rate the extension. It is shown at most twice, can be dismissed for good, offers no reward, and the counters are never sent anywhere.

What we do not do

We do not collect, sell or share your data, use it for advertising, use it to judge creditworthiness, or transfer it to anyone. ScrollTop does not load or run remote code.

Permissions

  • storage: to save your settings.

  • activeTab: so the toolbar popup can show the address of the current site and switch the button off for it.

  • Access to http and https pages: so the button can appear on the pages you read.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date above changes and the new text is published at the same address.
